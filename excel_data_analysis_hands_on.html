Data types & cell formatting
Format your data correctly so Excel can calculate it properly.
Raw data entered in Excel has no "format" by default — it's just generic. You must tell Excel: is this a number? Currency? Text? If column E (Unit Price) is stored as text, the SUM formula won't work. Formatting is step one of any clean analysis.
The 4 formats you'll use today
Format 1
Text (General)
Use for names and categories. Columns A (Rep Name), B (Region), C (Product) should stay as General/Text. Never apply number formatting to names.
Format 2
Number (0 decimal places)
Use for counts. Column D (Units Sold) — select D2:D11, press Ctrl+1, choose Number, set decimals to 0.
Format 3
Accounting / Currency
Use for money. Columns E (Unit Price) and F (Total Sales) — select E2:E11, press Ctrl+1, choose Accounting, set symbol to ₦ or leave as None.
Format 4
Bold header row
Select row 1 (click the "1" on the left side), press Ctrl+B to bold all headers. Then Home → Fill Color → choose a light green or blue shade to highlight your header row.

Writing formulas — the heart of Excel
Calculate Total Sales, then summarise the whole dataset.
Every formula in Excel starts with =. Excel then reads the rest as a calculation. You'll use 5 essential formulas on your dataset right now — each one answers a real business question.
Formula 1 — Multiplication: fill column F (Total Sales)
=D2*E2
What it does: Multiplies Units Sold (D2) × Unit Price (E2) to get the total revenue for that row.

How to use it: Click cell F2, type =D2*E2, press Enter. Then click F2 again → hover over the bottom-right corner until you see a small + sign → drag it down to F11. Excel copies the formula automatically for each row.

Formula 2 — SUM: total revenue for all reps
=SUM(F2:F11)
What it does: Adds up every number in column F from row 2 to row 11.
Where to put it: Click an empty cell below your data, e.g. F13. Label it in E13: type "Total Revenue".
Formula 3 — AVERAGE: average units sold per rep
=AVERAGE(D2:D11)
What it does: Calculates the mean number of units sold across all 10 reps.
Where to put it: Cell D13. Label it in C13: "Avg Units".
Formula 4 — MAX and MIN: best and lowest sales
=MAX(F2:F11)      =MIN(F2:F11)
MAX finds the highest Total Sales value (best performer). MIN finds the lowest.
Where to put them: F14 for MAX, F15 for MIN. Label them in column E.
Formula 5 — COUNT: how many reps in the dataset
=COUNTA(A2:A11)
What it does: Counts non-empty cells — tells you there are 10 reps. Use COUNTA (not COUNT) for text columns.
Where to put it: A13. Label it in A12: "Total Reps".

Create your first chart
Turn your numbers into a visual story that anyone can understand.
Charts make your analysis communicate instantly. A table of 10 rows takes 30 seconds to read. A bar chart tells the same story in 3 seconds. We'll build two charts: one comparing total sales per rep, and one showing sales by product.
Chart 1 — Clustered Bar: Sales by Rep
1
Hold Ctrl and select column A (A1:A11) — this is your rep names
2
Still holding Ctrl, also select column F (F1:F11) — total sales
3
Go to Insert tab → click Bar Chart → choose Clustered Bar
4
A chart appears on your sheet — click on the chart title and rename it: "Total Sales by Rep"
5
Right-click the chart → Format Chart Area → choose a clean background colour
Which chart for which data? Use Bar/Column to compare categories. Use Line for trends over time. Use Pie/Doughnut for proportions.

Chart 2 — Doughnut: Sales share by Product

Then select H1:I5 → Insert → Doughnut Chart. This shows each product's share of total revenue.

Bonus challenge — try these on your own
Bonus 1
=COUNTIF(B2:B11,"Lagos")
Counts how many reps are in Lagos. Try it for other regions too. COUNTIF counts cells that meet a condition.
Bonus 2
=SUMIF(C2:C11,"Laptop",F2:F11)
Adds up Total Sales only for Laptop rows. SUMIF is one of the most powerful beginner formulas — condition-based summing.
Bonus 3
Conditional Formatting
Select F2:F11 → Home → Conditional Formatting → Color Scales. Excel automatically highlights your highest values in green and lowest in red — instant visual ranking without a chart.
